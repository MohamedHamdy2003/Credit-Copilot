1. What You Will Build
Delta Commercial Bank (a fictional bank) offers personal loans to salaried customers. For every application, a credit officer must find the right edition of the credit policy, read the applicant’s documents, calculate the monthly installment and debt burden, check eligibility rules, and write a credit memo. It is slow, and mistakes either lose good customers or create bad loans.
Build a Credit Copilot Lite: it ingests the bank’s policy documents, answers policy questions with citations, and prepares a credit recommendation that a human credit officer approves or rejects.
Four rules your system must follow:
Grounded, never guessing. Every answer cites the document chunk it came from. If the documents don’t answer the question, the system must say so.
The numbers are never generated. The LLM never calculates installments, ratios or limits. Normal, tested functions compute every number.
Fair by design. Protected attributes (gender, religion, marital status, nationality) are removed before any LLM call or rule check, and a test proves they cannot change the outcome.
A human approves. No offer is “issued” until a credit officer approves it.
We are not expecting a finished product. We are looking for correct fundamentals, clean code, real tests, and honest documentation. A small system that works and that you can explain beats a large one you can’t.
This is a training exercise on synthetic data. Nothing in it is real lending policy or financial advice.
2. The Domain

2.3 Underwriting rules
Your credit policy defines the actual values. Your calculation and rule functions must implement:
Rule
Definition
1. Monthly installment
Reducing-balance formula: installment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where P = loan amount, r = annual rate ÷ 12, n = months. Rounded to 2 decimals, half-up.
2. Debt burden ratio (DBR)
DBR = (existing monthly obligations + new installment) ÷ net monthly income. Must be ≤ the policy maximum. Compare the exact value; display to 2 decimals.
3. Maximum eligible amount
The largest P whose installment keeps DBR at the maximum, rounded down to the nearest 1,000 EGP.
4. Age at maturity
Age at application + tenor in years ≤ the policy maximum (e.g., 60).
5. Employment & bureau
Minimum months with current employer and minimum bureau score from the policy. Below the score threshold, “Refer to human," not automatic decline.
6. Amount & tenor limits
The requested amount and tenor must be within the product sheet limits.

Worked example. Net income 30,000; existing obligations 4,000; request 300,000 over 60 months at 24% a year (r = 2% a month) → installment 8,630.39; DBR = 12,630.39 ÷ 30,000 = 42.10% ≤ 50% → pass. Maximum eligible amount at 50% (installment cap 11,000) = 382,000 EGP. Your unit tests must include this case.
2.4 Application assessment flow
A fixed pipeline; the steps always run in this order. You do not need autonomous agents.
#
Step
What happens
1
Load application
Read the application form; validate it against a schema (amount, tenor, date of birth, application date).
2
Remove protected attributes
Strip gender, religion, marital status, and nationality. Code, not the LLM. Log that it happened.
3
Find the policy edition.
Pick the credit policy edition in force on the application date. This is code, not the LLM.
4
Extract applicant data
The LLM reads the salary certificate and bureau summary and returns JSON (schema-validated): net income, obligations, employment start, and bureau score, each with the document and text it came from. Code checks that each value really appears in the cited text.
5
Retrieve policy clauses.
Search only the selected edition (and current circulars) for the limits that apply.
6
Calculate & check rules.
Your functions (Section 2.3) compute installment, DBR, maximum amount, and all rule results: pass/fail/refer.
7
Draft credit memo
The LLM writes a short memo from the rule results with policy citations; every number is inserted by code, not typed by the LLM.
8
Approval
The recommendation is saved as pending. A credit officer approves or rejects it with a comment. Only then is the offer marked Issued.

If any step fails or evidence is missing, the result is “Refer to human", never a guess.
3. Functional Requirements
FR-1 Ingestion. Separate functions for extract → chunk → embed → index. Each chunk stores: source file, page, section/clause ID, policy edition, and effective dates. Running ingestion twice must not create duplicates. Show which documents succeeded or failed.
FR-2 Retrieval & Q&A. Vector (semantic) search is required. Adding keyword search (e.g. BM25) and combining the two is a stretch goal, if you do it, report the evaluation numbers before and after. Filter by policy edition when a question or application implies one. Every answer lists its citations. Below a relevance threshold, answer: “The documents do not contain enough information.”
FR-3 Application assessment. The pipeline in Section 2.4, runnable from the API and the UI/CLI.
FR-4 Calculation & Rules Engine. Pure functions with no database, network, or LLM calls. Unit-tested against the worked example and edge cases (DBR exactly at the limit, zero existing obligations, age exactly at the limit, amount at the product minimum and maximum).
FR-5 Approval. Pending → Approved/Rejected → Issued. Store who approved, when, and their comment. A Credit Officer cannot approve above their authority limit (e.g. 250,000 EGP)  the server rejects it.
FR-6 Evaluation. A test set of 15 questions with expected answers: ≥3 out-of-corpus (must refuse), ≥2 where the answer differs between policy editions, ≥3 calculation cases, ≥2 prompt injection (≥1 hidden inside an applicant document). A script prints: retrieval hit-rate, refusal correctness, calculation exactness. Record your real results, including failures, and explain them.
FR-7 Interface. A REST API with OpenAPI docs (auto-generated is fine) and a minimal web UI or CLI to: ingest, ask a question, assess an application, approve/reject. No time on styling.
FR-8 Access. Login with two roles  Loan Officer (ask, submit applications) and Credit Officer (also approve/reject). Checked on the server, not by hiding buttons.
FR-9 Logging. Each request gets an ID. For each assessment, store the steps executed, the chunk IDs retrieved, the policy edition used, the fields removed in step 2, and the tokens consumed.
4. Code & Engineering
Layered structure. Keep domain logic (application, policy, calculations, rules) separate from infrastructure (LLM, database, vector store, web). The calculation engine and domain code must not import any LLM or vector-store library.
LLM behind an interface. One interface (e.g. complete, embed) with one working implementation using a free API tier. Changing the provider must need only configuration plus one new adapter class  no edits to domain or pipeline code. Explain in DESIGN.md exactly which file someone would add to switch provider. A second working implementation (e.g. a local model via Ollama) is a stretch goal.
Explicit errors. Model expected failures as named error types (e.g. PolicyEditionNotFound, InvalidApplication, UnverifiedExtraction, InvalidLLMOutput, AuthorityLimitExceeded) and turn each into a clear API response and, where relevant, a “Refer to human” result  no bare exceptions or silent failures.
Configuration in environment variables with a complete .env.example. Prompts stored in files, not hard-coded strings.
Storage: any relational database (SQLite is fine) + any vector store (pgvector, Chroma, Qdrant, …). The relational schema is created and changed through migrations (e.g. Alembic, EF Core, Flyway, Prisma), never by ad-hoc code at startup.
Dependencies pinned, with the lockfile committed (e.g. poetry.lock / uv.lock, package-lock.json, packages.lock.json).
Code style enforced by a linter and formatter (e.g. Ruff, ESLint + Prettier, dotnet format), configured in the repository and run in CI.
Tests (quality over quantity): calculation and rule unit tests; pipeline tests with the LLM replaced by a fake (covering at least an approvable application, an over-age application, and invalid LLM output → “Refer to human”); a fairness test showing that changing only protected attributes never changes the result; one integration test for ingestion + retrieval; a test that LLM JSON not matching the schema is rejected. The pipeline tests must run without internet or API keys.
Run it: docker compose up (or one documented command) starts everything; one command loads the demo documents.
5. Security Basics
No API keys or passwords in the repository  including Git history. Check before submitting (e.g. gitleaks).
Applicant documents are untrusted: pass them to the LLM clearly separated from your instructions, and never let their text change what the system does. Show this working in your evaluation (S3).
Mask national ID numbers and phone numbers before sending text to the LLM. Note in the README exactly what data is sent to the LLM provider.
Validate uploads (file type and size). Use parameterised database queries. Never render LLM output as raw HTML.

Appendix A  Lending Terms
Term
Meaning
Underwriting
Deciding whether to lend, how much, and on what terms.
Principal / tenor
The amount borrowed; the repayment period in months.
Reducing-balance rate
Interest charged on the remaining balance each month; gives a fixed monthly installment.
Installment (EMI)
The fixed monthly payment.
Net monthly income
Salary after tax and deductions, from the salary certificate.
Existing obligations
Monthly payments on the applicant’s current loans and cards, from the bureau report.
Debt burden ratio (DBR)
Share of income that goes to debt payments, including the new loan.
Credit bureau score
A number summarising repayment history (synthetic in this task).
Age at maturity
The applicant’s age when the last installment is due.
Credit policy edition
The version of the bank’s lending rules; the one in force on the application date applies.
Authority limit
The largest loan a credit officer may approve alone.
Protected attributes
Personal characteristics that must not influence a lending decision.

Appendix C  Where to Start
If retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is new to you, these are enough to begin. Budget about half a day for reading and a first experiment.
Topic
What to look for
What RAG is
Search your documents first, then give the found text to the model and ask it to answer only from that text. Start with any official “RAG quickstart” from a vector database (Chroma, Qdrant or pgvector all have one).
Embeddings
Turning text into vectors so similar meanings sit near each other. The embeddings guide of whichever model provider you use is enough.
Chunking
Splitting documents into pieces before embedding. For this pack, split by clause or section (CP-4.1, PM-2, …) rather than by a fixed number of characters  and explain your choice in DESIGN.md.
Structured output
Ask the model for JSON and validate it (Pydantic, zod, FluentValidation, …). Look up “structured output” or “JSON mode” in your provider’s documentation.
Prompt injection
Read the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications entry on prompt injection. It is SQL injection’s cousin: untrusted text ending up where instructions are read.
Free model access
Most providers have a free tier; a local model through Ollama also works. Your code must not depend on which one you chose.
A practical order of work: (1) load the documents and get search working; (2) add citations and the “not enough information” answer; (3) write the calculation functions and their tests; (4) connect the pipeline with fake LLM output; (5) plug in the real model; (6) add roles and approval; (7) write the 15 evaluation cases last, and fix what they reveal.

Appendix D  Questions We Expect You to Ask
Question
Answer
How does an application get into the system?
Your choice, and you must state it in DESIGN.md. Any of these is acceptable: upload the application PDF, select one of the five packs already loaded by the seed command, or type the key fields into a form. Whatever you choose, the five provided packs must be assessable end to end, and the documents must go to the untrusted store, not the policy knowledge base.
Are the 15 evaluation questions provided?
No. You write them yourself from the provided documents, with the answer you expect. Writing good test cases is part of what we are assessing.
What exactly is “retrieval hit-rate”?
The share of your test questions where at least one of the top-k retrieved chunks contains the text needed to answer correctly. State the k you used (5 is a reasonable default) and how you judged “contains”.
Where does the interest rate come from?
From the pricing table (CSV), selected by tenor band and segment  standard, unless the application says salary is transferred to Delta. The rate must never be hard-coded in your source.
How do I “verify” a number the LLM extracted?
Normalise both sides (strip commas, currency symbols and spaces) and check that the value appears in the text the model cited. If it does not, raise UnverifiedExtraction and refer the application to a human. Do not silently trust the number.
Can I use LangChain, LlamaIndex or a similar framework?
Yes, but you must be able to explain what every part of it does in your code. A framework that hides your pipeline from you will hurt you in the interview.
Which model should I use?
Any free API tier, or a local model. Your code must work through your own interface so the choice can change.
Do I need OCR?
No. Every provided PDF has a real text layer.
Do I need Arabic support?
No. The documents are in English.
What if two documents disagree?
Answer with the rule that applies, cite both, and explain which one wins and why (a later circular supersedes an earlier one; an internal cap can be stricter than the regulator’s).
